# World 5: Market Math (Planned)

**Big idea:** Small price moves can add up to large dollar amounts because one contract covers a large quantity. Leverage makes gains AND losses bigger. Margin is not the most you can lose.

## Learning objectives

| | Grades 3–5 | Grades 6–8 |
|---|---|---|
| Differences | Whole-dollar and simple cents differences. | Decimal differences to hundredths. |
| Percent | "Out of 100" with friendly numbers (10%, 50%). | (new − old) ÷ old × 100; compare changes on different prices. |
| Ticks/contract size | A tick is the smallest price step; one contract covers a lot of something. | Tick value = tick size × contract size; count ticks between prices (pretend contracts unless verified). |
| Gains/losses | Price move × amount = money gained or lost, both directions. | Long and short P/L with pretend contracts; show losses as often as gains. |
| Margin/leverage | Margin is a deposit, NOT the most you can lose. | Margin as performance bond (2–10% of value, CFTC); leverage magnifies losses; margin calls; losses can exceed the deposit. |
