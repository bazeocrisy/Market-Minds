# World 4: Reading Markets (Planned)

**Big idea:** Charts show what prices did in the past. They help us describe and ask questions; they do not tell us what will happen next. Being long means you are helped by a rise and hurt by a fall; being short is the reverse.

## Learning objectives

| | Grades 3–5 | Grades 6–8 |
|---|---|---|
| Line charts | Find highest/lowest point; say up, down or flat. | Describe trend; compute change between points; distinguish trend from prediction. |
| Candlesticks | A candle shows start, end, highest and lowest price. | Read open/high/low/close; up vs. down candles; range = high − low. |
| Long/short | Buyer side helped if price rises; seller side helped if price falls. | Long = bought futures position; short = selling side (CFTC glossary); gains and losses for each with pretend prices. |
| Limits | A chart can't see the future. | Patterns can fail; news and surprises move prices; no chart method guarantees results. |

## Prerequisite knowledge

w1l2 (line charts, price change), w2l1 (buyer/seller of an agreement).

## Vocabulary

line chart, trend, candlestick, open/high/low/close, long, short, uncertainty.

## Required visuals

`line`, `candles` (pretend data), long/short outcome table (HTML).

## Practice question ideas

Chart reading (highest, lowest, change); candle OHLC identification; long/short "who is helped?" scenarios; mistake detective: "The line went up 3 days, so it must go up tomorrow."

## Assessment plan

Lesson checks; World 4 Test with chart items; Thinking Lab: "What could make this chart turn around?"

## Accuracy notes

- Long/short definitions verified against the CFTC Glossary.
- Candlestick/OHLC conventions are standard but **not checked against a fetched authority** (see SOURCES.md); verify before writing lessons.
- The curriculum does **not** teach any indicator-based signal method (e.g. RSI signals). Charts are taught as description, not prediction.
