# World 2: The Future Price (Partial: 1 of 4 ready)

**Big idea:** Nobody knows future prices. Farmers worry prices will fall; bakers worry prices will rise. An agreement made today about a future price can reduce that uncertainty, but it can turn out better or worse, and it never guarantees a profit.

## Teaching sequence in w2l1

The lesson builds the **why** before the vocabulary:
1. Discover: the farmer's worry (price could fall before harvest) and the baker's worry (price could rise). Same uncertainty, opposite sides.
2. Definition: their handshake deal is a **forward agreement**. Only then: hedging, and (explorer) "real futures contracts are like this kind of promise, traded on special markets called exchanges with rules"; (analyst) futures are standardized, exchange-traded, terms set by the exchange, most positions offset before delivery (CFTC).
3. Visual: the agreement card.
4. Together: two harvest scenarios (price lower vs. higher) and "did they know in advance?"
5. Check: explorer — who did the agreement help? analyst — which statement about futures is accurate?

## Practice question ideas

- `future-agreement`: identify what is agreed now vs. what happens later; forward vs. futures (analyst); "which is NOT true about futures" (guaranteed profit, custom terms, always delivery).
- `hedge-outcome`: given agreed and market price, who benefited; per-unit difference; analyst total = difference × quantity; "both win?" mistake detective.
- `futures-risk`: "Could they know in advance?"; list one upside and one downside of agreeing early; analyst: hedging gives up the chance of a better price.

## Accuracy notes (verified, see SOURCES.md)

- Futures contracts "typically are traded on organized exchanges that set standardized terms"; exchanges set contract size, delivery months, last trading day, delivery locations and grades. (CFTC, *Economic Purpose*)
- "Most futures contracts (by volume) are liquidated by offsetting and do not result in delivery." (CFTC)
- A CBOT wheat contract provides for delivery of 5,000 bushels. (CFTC example; used for analyst quantity)
- The farmer/baker deal is a **forward agreement**, not a futures contract, and the content says so.
- No content claims hedging guarantees profit; both upside and downside scenarios are shown.
