# World 1: Money & Markets (Ready)

**Big idea:** Markets are wherever buyers and sellers trade. Prices change for reasons, and supply and demand push prices up or down.

## Lessons

| Id | Title | Status | Skills |
|---|---|---|---|
| w1l1 | What Is a Market? | Ready | `market-basics`, `buyers-sellers` |
| w1l2 | Why Prices Change | Ready | `price-change-math`, `price-change-reasons` |
| w1l3 | Supply and Demand | Ready | `supply-demand` |

## Learning objectives

| Lesson | Grades 3–5 (Explorer) | Grades 6–8 (Analyst) |
|---|---|---|
| w1l1 | Say what a market is; point out buyer and seller; tell what each wants. | Define a market (physical or digital); identify buyers, sellers, goods, services; explain that a trade needs both sides to agree. |
| w1l2 | Find a price change by subtracting (whole dollars); read a simple line chart; give one reason prices change. | Calculate dollar and percent change; describe a line-chart trend; explain weather, cost and demand as reasons, and that charts show the past, not the future. |
| w1l3 | Explain supply and demand; predict up/down price pressure. | Define supply, demand, shortage, surplus; predict and justify price pressure; recognize "it depends" when both change. |

## Prerequisite knowledge

- Grades 3–5: counting money in whole dollars; subtraction within 100.
- Grades 6–8: decimal subtraction and division; percent as "out of 100".
- Lesson order: w1l1 → w1l2 → w1l3 (w1l3 lists `market-basics` and `price-change-reasons` as prereqs).

## Practice question ideas (for the engine)

- `market-basics`: "Is this a market?" scenarios (online shop, library, closet, yard sale); vocab matching (market/goods/services).
- `buyers-sellers`: identify buyer/seller in a short scenario; what each side wants; same person in both roles.
- `price-change-math`: number entry for new − old; up/down direction; analyst percent change (divide by OLD price); read change from a line chart.
- `price-change-reasons`: pick a plausible reason (weather, shipping cost, popularity, new model); mistake detective ("prices always rise in winter").
- `supply-demand`: scenario → price direction; identify shortage vs. surplus; analyst "both change → unclear".

Common misconceptions to target: market must be a building; buyer/seller swap; adding instead of subtracting; dividing by the new price; less supply → lower price; more buyers → lower price; prices are fixed.

## Accuracy notes

- Supply/demand outcomes are always phrased as tendencies ("tends to", "most likely"), because real prices also depend on costs, competition and rules. See `SOURCES.md` (supply/demand wording not checked against a fetched authority).
- All prices are pretend and labeled as such in chart titles.
